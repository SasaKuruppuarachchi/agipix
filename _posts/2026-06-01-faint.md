FAINT (Fast, Appearance-Invariant Navigation Transformer) asks whether visual navigation policies trained in simulation can match policies trained on real-world data. In tests on a wheeled robot, the simulator-trained policy beat its real-world-trained version by 31% and prior state-of-the-art methods by 50% in navigation success rate.

## How AgiPIX was used

To test whether the policy carries over to a different kind of robot, the authors took the model trained with a simulated wheeled robot and deployed it, without modification, on an AgiPIX drone. The drone carried a forward-facing 110° field-of-view USB camera and a Jetson Orin NX, and all computation ran on board.

The policy controlled forward velocity, up to 0.4 m/s, and yaw rate at a fixed height of about 1.5 m, on two open-space routes. The drone reached the goal on one route with 1.07 m RMSE and missed it on a more challenging one, at 1.42 m RMSE. Inference took 120 ms on the Orin NX. The paper describes these drone results as preliminary and leaves a fuller analysis to future work.
